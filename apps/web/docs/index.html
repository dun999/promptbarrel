Documentation for Barrel.

Barrel holds prepaid AI inference in a NEAR contract and pays the operator per answer, only when the model's own TEE has signed it. This page covers using it, handing it to an agent, the protocol underneath, and running an operator.

Start
01

Overview

Barrel sells prepaid inference from named models. You pay USDC into a NEAR contract and receive Barrels for one model's series. The operator who serves the model is paid out of that escrow per answer, and only after the contract has checked two signatures: the model's, made inside its TEE over the exact request and answer, and your client's acknowledgement of that answer. Whatever you do not spend comes back to you.

No proof, no ack. No ack, no payment.A cheaper model swapped in, or one changed byte in the answer, fails the check, and the units stay in your tank.
  1. 1Buy

    USDC becomes Barrels of one model's series. The USDC waits in the contract's escrow.

  2. 2Fill

    Barrels go into a tank under a spend key that can pay for answers and nothing else.

  3. 3Ask

    The request goes to the operator's Pump and on to the model's TEE on NEAR AI Cloud, in plaintext or encrypted to the model.

  4. 4Settle

    Your client checks the model's signature and signs an ack. The contract checks both and pays.

  5. 5Refund

    After the series closes, unused Barrels burn for the USDC behind them.

Barrel runs on NEAR Protocol (the contracts, the escrow, native ed25519_verify, and Intel quote verification on chain), NEAR AI Cloud (models in Intel TDX and NVIDIA confidential computing, with per-answer signatures and end-to-end encryption), and NEAR Intents (USDC from other chains, and resale).

02

Quick start

You need a NEAR account in MyNearWallet, HOT or Intear with a little NEAR for gas and the 0.01 NEAR tank deposit, and USDC, either on NEAR or on another chain. An agent needs less: a key, and someone to fund it (see HTTP 402 and x402).

  1. 01
    Connect

    Open the app and connect your wallet. Every write is a transaction your wallet shows you first.

  2. 02
    Buy Barrels

    Pick a model's series, enter an amount of USDC and sign. Or pay with USDC on another chain: the app gets you a deposit address from NEAR Intents and the Barrels land in your NEAR account.

  3. 03
    Fill a tank

    Your browser makes a fresh spend key and keeps it in local storage. Your wallet signs fill with the key's public half and a 0.01 NEAR storage deposit.

  4. 04
    Ask

    The browser signs the request with the tank key and sends it to the series' Pump. Tick Private to encrypt it to the model. It checks the model's signature itself and with the contract's free verify_receipt view, and only then signs the ack. The app links the settlement transaction.

  5. 05
    Hand it to an agent

    The app shows a claude mcp add command with the tank id and key filled in, and offers the key as a file.

  6. 06
    Take the rest back

    Unlock a tank and withdraw its Barrels after 10 minutes. After the series closes, refund unused Barrels for their USDC.

Before you buy

Asking needs the series' Pump at an address your browser can reach: a public HTTPS address, or this machine. The app marks a series whose Pump it cannot reach before you buy, and Deployments below shows where each series points right now.

03

Concepts

Barrel
1,000,000 units of inference from one series. A NEP-245 token whose id is the series id, with 6 decimals, so one Barrel reads as 1.0.
Unit
What an answer is metered in: one completion token, plus a weighted share of each prompt token.
Grade
A model id and its prompt-token weight, for example glm-5.3-flash → z-ai/glm-5.3-flash at 16%. Model signing keys are registered per grade.
Series
One operator selling one grade at a fixed price until a closing time, with a cap on units and the address of its Pump. Anyone can open a series.
Firm series
A series whose operator has posted a USDC bond and must answer requests sent through the chain before a deadline, or pay from the bond.
Escrow
The USDC behind minted Barrels. It leaves only as operator pay for a proven answer, as a refund, or as a job tank's payout to its agent.
Tank
Barrels set aside under a spend key. The account that filled it owns it, or the key itself when the tank was bought for a key.
Spend key
An Ed25519 key made for one tank. It signs requests and acks. It cannot transfer, withdraw or refund.
Job tank
A tank filled for another account, the agent. If the owner accepts the work, the leftover is paid to the agent in USDC.
Pump
The operator's gateway. It serves funded tanks, relays the exact bytes to NEAR AI Cloud and back, and settles acks on chain.
Receipt
The model id, the hashes of the request and the answer, and the model's signing key and signature over them.
Ack
The spend key's signature over the contract, tank, sequence number, both hashes and the units billed.
Claim
The Pump's way to be paid for an answer nobody acknowledged: the payer's own signed ceiling and the model's signature. The payer's ack still wins inside the claim window.
Attestor
A contract that checks a model's Intel TDX quote on chain and registers its signing key. Anyone can call it.
Index
The volume-weighted price per Barrel of each model over the last 24 hours, counted only from deliveries the contract verified.
Use
04

Buying Barrels

With USDC on NEAR

A buy is one USDC transfer to the contract, ft_transfer_call with the message {"series_id": 1}. The contract mints amount × 1,000,000 ÷ price units to you, or to the account named in "to". At 0.60 USDC per Barrel, 1 USDC buys 1.666666 Barrels. Any part of the amount that would go past the series cap comes back in the same transaction, and a buy past the contract's total escrow cap is refused and returned.

Straight into a key's tank

With {"series_id": 1, "tank_key": "<64 hex>"} the Barrels go into a tank owned by that Ed25519 key, with no NEAR account in between. Anyone can pay; only the key can spend the tank. Paying again with the same key tops the tank up. The key can later hand the tank to a NEAR account with assign, which anyone can relay. A purchase must buy at least 10,000 units, so nobody can fill the contract with dust tanks.

With USDC on another chain

The app and the CLI quote a NEAR Intents 1Click swap whose recipient is the Barrel contract and whose message is {"series_id": N, "to": "<your NEAR account>"}, or {"series_id": N, "tank_key": "…"} to fund a key. You send one deposit on the origin chain; 1Click swaps it to USDC on NEAR and delivers it with ft_transfer_call, and the contract mints the Barrels. You never hold USDC on NEAR, and the operator never holds your money.

  • An EVM wallet in the browser can send the deposit for you; on any other chain, send the quoted amount to the deposit address yourself.
  • A swap not finished by the quote's deadline is refunded to your address on the origin chain.
  • Quotes are kept under the series' room and the global escrow cap, because a delivery the contract refuses goes back to NEAR Intents rather than to you.
  • Without a 1Click JWT (ONECLICK_JWT, CLI and Pump) 1Click adds a 0.25% fee.
pnpm barrel buy-from base 1 1.0 --refund-to 0xYourBaseAddress --account you.near
pnpm barrel fund-from base 1 1.0 --refund-to 0xYourBaseAddress     # into a new key's tank
05

Tanks and spend keys

fill(series_id, units, key, agent?) moves Barrels from your balance into a new tank under the public half of a spend key, with a 0.01 NEAR storage deposit. In the app the key is made in your browser and kept in its local storage for this site, per contract; you can download it as a file, and import a key from a file or from .barrel/tanks/. The CLI writes keys to .barrel/tanks/<contract>/<tank>.json, and keys made with barrel key to .barrel/keys/.

Answers settle strictly in order: a tank has a next sequence number, and every request and ack names it. top_up(tank_id, units) adds Barrels and cancels an unlock in progress.

StateMeaning
ActiveThe Pump serves it. Its key can pay for proven answers.
Claim openThe Pump claimed an answer the key never acknowledged. Acknowledge it, or the claimed ceiling is paid when the claim window ends. No withdrawal meanwhile.
UnlockingThe owner asked for the rest back. The Pump no longer serves it. Withdraw after the unlock delay, or top up to keep using it.
ClosedWithdrawn, or a job tank whose leftover went to the agent or back to the owner.
06

Asking

The app, the CLI and the MCP server all ask the same way (TankClient in packages/sdk, and apps/web/src/tank.ts in the browser):

  1. Build a non-streamed chat request for the series' model with a max_tokens.
  2. Work out the most units the request could cost, sign the request text with the tank key, and send it to the series' Pump.
  3. Receive the model's exact answer bytes with a receipt and the units billed.
  4. Check that the signature is the model TEE's (provider_tee), that the signed text names the model and both hashes, that the hashes match the bytes sent and received, that the contract's verify_receipt finds the signature valid and the key registered and live, and that the units match the answer's usage.
  5. Only then sign the ack and send it. The Pump settles it on chain.

If any check fails, no ack is signed. The Pump serves a tank one answer at a time and will not serve it again until the last answer is settled. A payer who takes an answer and never acknowledges it does not get it free: after 2 minutes (the Pump's default) it files a claim with the payer's own request signature, and when the claim window (5 minutes) ends, the ceiling the payer signed is paid. An ack sent before then replaces the claim and the true units are charged.

Privately

Tick Private in the app, pass --private to barrel ask, or set BARREL_PRIVATE=1 for the MCP server. Each message is encrypted to the newest live key the contract has registered for the model, the same key that signs answers, and the model encrypts its answer back to the tank key (NEAR AI Cloud's end-to-end encryption: X25519 from the Ed25519 keys, HKDF-SHA256, XChaCha20-Poly1305). The Pump relays ciphertext. The model signs the ciphertext bytes, so payment works exactly as before: the client checks the signature, checks that the key it encrypted to is the key that signed, decrypts locally, and acknowledges. Token counts stay readable, so metering does not change.

07

Job tanks

Fund an agent and pay it what it does not spend. A client fills a tank naming another account as its agent and gives the agent the spend key. The agent pays for its own proven inference out of the tank while it works. Every answer it paid for is on chain with the model's signature, so the client sees what the job cost before deciding.

CallWhoEffect
fill(series, units, key, agent)ClientFunds the job. The agent gets the spend key.
ask … settleAgent, PumpEach answer is paid on proof.
accept(tank)ClientAccepts the work. The Pump stops serving the tank.
withdraw(tank)Client or agentAfter the unlock delay, pays the leftover's USDC to the agent.
request_unlock(tank)ClientRejects the work. After the unlock delay the leftover comes back to the client.

The agent's account must be registered with the USDC token to receive it. If it is not, the payout bounces, the tank is restored and nothing is lost; the app offers to open the agent's USDC account (storage_deposit) and pay again.

08

Withdrawals and refunds

  • Unlock a tank. request_unlock(tank), then withdraw(tank) after the unlock delay (10 minutes) returns its Barrels to your balance. The delay is longer than the claim window, so answers already served settle first.
  • Refund Barrels. After the series closes and the settle grace (1 hour) has passed, refund(series, units) burns Barrels for floor(units × price ÷ 1,000,000) USDC. Series 1 closes Dec 31, 2026, 00:00 UTC. If a firm series defaults, refunds open at once and add a share of the bond.
  • Move Barrels. Barrels in your account are NEP-245 tokens: mt_transfer and mt_transfer_call work as the standard says.
  • Owed payouts. A payout the token refuses is kept on chain as owed; claim_owed collects it.
09

Resale

Barrels are NEP-245 tokens, so they trade inside NEAR Intents (intents.near) with no exchange contract of Barrel's own. A seller deposits Barrels and signs one intent: give these Barrels, get this much USDC. A buyer with USDC deposited signs the mirror image. Anyone submits both to execute_intents, and both sides move in one transaction or nothing moves.

# sell 0.5 Barrels of series 1 for 0.25 USDC
pnpm barrel sell 1 500000 0.25 --account seller.near
pnpm barrel offers 1
pnpm barrel take <offer id> --account buyer.near
# take the Barrels back out: the offer can no longer settle
pnpm barrel unsell 1 --account seller.near
  • An offer is a signed NEP-413 message with a token_diff intent, a nonce from the Verifier's salt and a deadline. The Pump's /v1/offers relay only carries offers; the buyer checks the signed terms, never the relay's summary.
  • The Verifier keeps a small fee of what each side receives (1 pip on mainnet today). take runs the free simulate_intents view first and leaves the fee out of the units it asks for, so nothing moves until the trade balances.
  • Mainnet only: NEAR Intents does not run on testnet.

MCP server

apps/mcp is an MCP server over stdio that gives an agent one tank. The agent holds only the tank's spend key, so the most it can do is spend the tank on answers that carry a valid model signature. With a tank filled in the app, use the command it shows:

claude mcp add barrel \
  -e BARREL_NETWORK=mainnet \
  -e BARREL_CONTRACT=barrel-v2.promptbarrel.near \
  -e BARREL_TANK=<tank id> \
  -e BARREL_TANK_KEY=<spend key> \
  -- node /path/to/barrel/apps/mcp/bin/barrel-mcp.mjs

An agent with only a key leaves out BARREL_TANK: it finds the tank its key owns, and until someone funds one, barrel_balance tells it exactly how anyone can.

HTTP 402 and x402

An agent with a key and nothing else sends its request with x-barrel-key: <public key>. If the key owns no funded tank, the Pump answers 402 Payment Required with an x402 body: how much to pay, where, and the exact message that puts the purchase into a tank only that key can spend. The money goes to the Barrel contract's escrow, never to the operator.

{
  "x402Version": 1,
  "error": "this key has no funded tank",
  "accepts": [{
    "scheme": "exact",
    "network": "near-mainnet",
    "asset": "<USDC contract>",
    "payTo": "<Barrel contract>",
    "maxAmountRequired": "1000000",
    "resource": "https://<pump>/v1/chat/completions",
    "extra": {
      "method": "ft_transfer_call",
      "msg": "{\"series_id\":1,\"tank_key\":\"<key>\"}",
      "tank": "https://<pump>/v1/tank?key=<key>",
      "min_amount": "6000"
    }
  }]
}
  • x-barrel-pay: <USDC base units> asks for a different amount. On mainnet, x-barrel-pay-from: <chain> and x-barrel-refund-to: <address> add a second offer: a NEAR Intents deposit address on that chain that delivers the same purchase into the same tank.
  • Once paid, GET /v1/tank?key=… returns the tank, and the agent signs requests with the key as usual. TankClient without a tankId does this lookup itself and throws PaymentRequired with the offers when there is nothing to spend.
  • A key tank that runs low gets the same 402 with an offer to top it up.

Why x402 funds a tank rather than paying per request: an x402 payment settles before the answer exists, while Barrel pays only after the model's signature over the finished answer checks out. Other 402 answers carry a plain JSON error: the tank is closed, accepted, unlocking, has a claim open, or the series has expired.

Models and pricing

units = completion_tokens + ⌈prompt_tokens × input_weight_bps ÷ 10,000⌉

Each model is a grade with its own series. The prompt-token weight is the model's input price over its output price, so a unit costs the same whether it was read or written; the series price is the output price per million tokens plus the operator's margin. Barrel offers the chat models that NEAR AI Cloud serves from its own attested TEEs, and a model is only listed if its attestation passes on the day it is added.

SeriesModelPer BarrelPrompt weight
Reading the contract…

An answer with 420 prompt and 180 completion tokens on z-ai/glm-5.3-flash (weight 16%) costs 180 + 68 = 248 units, about 0.00015 USDC at 0.60 per Barrel.

  • Before serving, the Pump requires a ceiling: the request's bytes counted as prompt tokens, plus max_tokens. The request signature commits to it, the tank must hold it, and the bill is the metered units, never more than the ceiling.
  • The operator is paid floor(cumulative_burned × price ÷ 1,000,000) minus what was already released, so rounding never adds up against either side.
  • Every proven delivery feeds the model's price index: get_index(grade) returns the volume-weighted price per Barrel over 24 hourly buckets.
14

Signatures

Four Ed25519 signatures, over four texts. Hashes are SHA-256 of the exact bytes, as 64 lowercase hex characters.

Model, inside its TEE

<model_id>:<sha256(request)>:<sha256(response)>

Request, by the tank key

barrel:v1:req:<contract>:<tank>:<seq>:<sha256(request)>:<max_units>

Ack, by the tank key

barrel:v1:ack:<contract>:<tank>:<seq>:<request_hash>:<response_hash>:<units>

Assign, by the tank key

barrel:v1:assign:<contract>:<tank>:<new_owner>

NEAR AI Cloud signs every non-streamed answer with the model's key. Streamed answers are signed by the gateway instead, over text that names no model, so the Pump refuses streaming and the contract would never pay a gateway signature: the gateway's key is not registered for any grade. The request signature is what a claim rests on, so a Pump can never claim more than the payer signed for. A receipt looks like this:

{
  "grade_id": "glm-5.3-flash",
  "model_id": "z-ai/glm-5.3-flash",
  "request_hash": "29809c43…2437",
  "response_hash": "20c229a6…3525",
  "signing_algo": "ed25519",
  "signing_address": "5a0210f2…caff",
  "signature": "49603d99…",
  "signature_kind": "provider_tee",
  "text": "z-ai/glm-5.3-flash:29809c43…:20c229a6…",
  "request_b64": "…",
  "response_b64": "…"
}

Check one in the browser on the app's Verify a receipt page, or with pnpm barrel verify <file>.

15

Settlement

settle(tank_id, items) takes 1 to 40 items. Each carries seq, request_hash, response_hash, units, ack_sig, tee_key and tee_sig. For each one, before any money moves, the contract checks:

  1. The sequence number is the tank's next one: no replay, no reordering.
  2. The ack verifies under the tank's spend key.
  3. The model key is registered for the series' grade and has not expired or been revoked.
  4. The model signature verifies over <model_id>:<request_hash>:<response_hash>, with model_id taken from the grade, not from the caller.
  5. The units fit in the tank.

Then it burns the units and pays the series operator. Signatures use NEAR's native ed25519_verify: the first item costs about 8.6 TGas including the payout, each further item about 1 TGas. Settlement is accepted until the series closes plus the settle grace.

Claims

claim_unacked(tank_id, seq, request_hash, max_units, auth_sig, response_hash, tee_key, tee_sig), operator only, reserves the ceiling the payer signed, after checking the payer's request signature and the model's signature. An ack for the same request through settle replaces the claim inside the claim window and charges the true units. After the window, finalize_claim(tank_id), which anyone can call, pays the ceiling.

16

Firm series

Paid-on-proof protects the buyer's money, not the buyer's time: an operator that goes quiet keeps the escrow locked until the series ends. A firm series closes that gap with a bond and requests the chain itself can see.

CallWhoEffect
BondOperatorA USDC ft_transfer_call with {"bond_series": N} adds to the series' bond.
force_requestTank ownerPuts a request on chain (exactly model, messages, max_tokens, up to 4,096 bytes) with a 0.25 NEAR deposit. The contract hashes it and reserves its worst case from the tank.
canaryAnyoneAsks the operator to prove it is serving. The contract writes the prompt from a random seed and pays the operator a small NEAR fee for an answer.
answer_forcedOperatorPosts the model's answer. The contract hashes it, checks the model's signature, reads the token counts out of the signed bytes and charges exactly that.
slash_forcedAnyone, after the deadlineThe reserve returns to the tank, the request's worst-case cost is paid to the tank owner from the bond, and it counts as a miss.
clear_forcedAnyone, once resolvedReturns the storage deposit.

At the series' miss limit it defaults: no more buying, refunds open at once, and each Barrel refunds with an equal share of what is left of the bond. A Pump answers forced requests and canaries on its own. The operator takes the bond back with withdraw_bond after settlement closes.

17

Attestation

The contract pays only signatures from keys registered for the grade, and on a deployment with an attestor only the attestor contract can register them. Anyone runs it: request_nonce(), fetch NEAR AI Cloud's report with that nonce, then register(grade_id, signing_key, nonce, quote, collateral, app_compose, event_log). The contract itself checks:

  • Intel DCAP verification of the TDX quote against Intel's collateral, with an accepted TCB status, on a non-debug TD.
  • The report data binds the signing key and the nonce the contract issued.
  • MRCONFIGID commits to the compose file, and the event log replays to RTMR3 with the same compose hash.
  • That compose hash is on the grade's reviewed list.

One check costs about 131 TGas, one per machine serving the model. The evidence hash is stored with the key, and keys expire after the grade's time to live (24 hours). A Pump re-registers a rotated key itself and pays the gas. The reviewed lists change only through the attestor's timelock. Collateral comes from Intel's own Provisioning Certification Service. Anyone can repeat the checks off chain too:

pnpm barrel attest z-ai/glm-5.3-flash    # → ATTESTED
pnpm barrel audit                        # registered keys vs. a fresh attestation
pnpm barrel register glm-5.3-flash       # register on chain, as anyone

The compose hash names NEAR AI Cloud's model-serving software, which is the same for several models, so it does not by itself say which model a key serves. What binds the model is the signed text: the TEE signs its own model id, and the contract checks the signature against the grade's model id, so a key from another model never produces a signature the grade accepts. NVIDIA GPU evidence is not checked yet. The checks follow NEAR AI's reference verifier, nearai-cloud-verifier.

Pump API

A Pump serves every series its operator runs, one per model. Clients normally use TankClient; this is the wire format underneath.

Or only x-barrel-key, to learn how to pay. Changes and upgrades

Nothing that moves trust changes at once. A new owner, a new attestor, or new code is first announced with propose(kind, value), visible to everyone through get_pending, and applies only after the timelock (24 hours) through apply, which anyone can call. New code is announced by its SHA-256; upgrade accepts only the exact bytes. A guardian, if set, can pause and cancel a pending change but can do nothing else. renounce_key_registration removes the owner's power to register keys for good, once an attestor is in place. The timelock binds the contract's own methods; a full-access key on the contract account could still replace the code directly, until that key is removed. NEAR's view_access_key_list on the contract account shows whether one is left.

Run a Pump

The operator runs one Pump for all its series. It needs the operator account's key in .barrel/mainnet/ and a NEAR AI Cloud API key as NEARAI_API_KEY in .env.

# serve it behind TLS at a public https:// address
PUMP_HOST=0.0.0.0 PUMP_PORT=8787 PUMP_PUBLIC_URL=https://pump.example.com pnpm pump

A browser reaches a Pump only over HTTPS, or on its own machine, so each series must point at a public HTTPS address before anyone else can ask. The operator (op-v2.promptbarrel.near) moves it by calling set_series_endpoint on the contract, Barrel.setSeriesEndpoint in packages/sdk. The endpoint only says where to ask; the payment rules do not change with it.

Answers not yet settled are kept in a journal under .barrel/pump/ and flushed to the chain on SIGINT or SIGTERM. Every 5 seconds the Pump files claims, finalizes them, and answers forced requests and canaries on its firm series. With an attestor, it re-registers a rotated model key itself.

To deploy: pnpm deploy:testnet, or pnpm deploy:mainnet --root <account> --endpoint https://… (prints the plan and its NEAR cost, and sends nothing without --yes). It attests every model on the list, deploys the contract and the attestor with the reviewed compose hashes it found, registers each key on chain, and opens one series per model. --models, --margin, --firm-bond, --timelock and --guardian change the defaults.

Trust and limits

What a receipt proves: a TEE holding a key registered for this model signed these exact request and answer bytes. What it does not:

Which machine served it

The receipt does not identify the serving instance.

Who registered the key

With an attestor, the chain checked Intel's quote before the key counted, against compose hashes someone reviewed. Without one, Barrel's registrar checks attestations off chain and publishes the evidence.

GPU evidence

NVIDIA GPU evidence is not checked yet. Guarantees rest on Intel TDX and NVIDIA confidential-computing assumptions.

Prompt privacy

Ask privately and the Pump relays ciphertext. It still sees token counts, timing and which tank asked. Without it, the Pump sees plaintext.

Availability

An operator can refuse to serve. The money stays safe either way; on a firm series, every unanswered forced request is paid from the bond, and a default opens refunds at once.

Real money, low caps

Mainnet escrow is capped at 50 USDC in total while the contract is new. Code and owner changes go through a public timelock, though the deploy key keeps full access to the contract account until it is removed.
